# Integrity-risk signal catalog

Signals are research priorities, not findings of misconduct.

## S1. Procurement concentration
A small set of suppliers receives an unusually high share of a unit's awards.

Check:
- HHI/share over time;
- procurement type and market concentration;
- whether concentration predates the subject;
- whether vendors are specialized or sole-qualified.

## S2. Post-arrival contract acceleration
A company materially increases awards after the subject assumes a relevant role.

Require:
- pre/post timeline;
- comparable procurement volume;
- authority fit;
- alternative explanation such as new factory/qualification/industry expansion.

## S3. Network migration / following network
A company, investor, intermediary or project network repeatedly appears in jurisdictions after the subject moves.

Strong version:
- repeated across multiple postings;
- entry shortly after arrival;
- government business rises in each jurisdiction;
- prior public relationship exists.

Do not infer improper patronage without more evidence.

## S4. Nominal competitor linkage
Repeated bidders share owners, historical owners, directors, addresses, phone contacts, project managers or other legally public connections.

Describe as "potential related-bidder signal" unless an authority has found collusion.

## S5. Project-change anomaly
Large or repeated contract amendments, cost increases, scope changes, delayed completion or repeated re-tendering.

Check technical and inflation explanations before escalating.

## S6. Land-project linkage
A land acquirer has prior/repeated meetings, investment ties, contracts or project relationships within the subject's authority network.

The existence of招商 meetings is usually normal; relevance rises only with transaction anomalies or conflicts.

## S7. SOE / financing-platform concentration
Repeated transactions with the same private counterparties, guarantees, asset sales, joint ventures or related-party structures involving local SOEs.

## S8. Personnel-business temporal overlap
A recurring professional associate later appears as owner/controller/executive of a government counterparty.

Require explicit identity matching; avoid same-name errors.

## S9. Disciplinary cluster
Multiple people from the same unit, project chain or tenure are separately investigated or convicted.

This justifies expanding the graph; it does **not** establish the subject's involvement.

## S10. Audit-repeat signal
The same issue appears repeatedly in audit findings without correction, especially in areas under the subject's authority.

## S11. Unexplained public-data mismatch
Conflicting official dates, project amounts, ownership identities or disclosure gaps.

Treat first as a data-quality issue; seek clarification/corroboration.

## S12. Repeated preferential contact + transaction
A private counterparty has unusually frequent one-on-one/restricted official contact **and** subsequent transactions within the subject's authority scope.

Public招商 contact alone is weak; transaction linkage makes it research-worthy.

# Signal severity
- **Low:** weak or generic pattern; mainly a search lead.
- **Medium:** multiple aligned public facts, but normal explanations remain strong.
- **High:** strong temporal/transactional alignment plus corroborating relationships or official adverse findings.

Never translate severity into guilt probability.
